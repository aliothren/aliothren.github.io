Research Experience

  • 06/2026-present
    Isobar: A Simulation Framework for Topology-Aware Mapping and Deployment Exploration in Large-Scale Analog Compute-in-Memory Systems
    University of Arizona
    • Developed Isobar to evaluate how physical weight mapping affects LLM execution on large-scale analog compute-in-memory systems, addressing communication bottlenecks obscured by local operator costs and aggregate traffic metrics.
    • Built a mapping-dependent execution model that derives communication from capacity-constrained matrix decompositions and heterogeneous analog-digital execution. Modeled contention on shared links, ports, and interfaces to distinguish mappings that aggregate communication metrics treat as equivalent.
    • Developed a phase analysis connecting CIM processing speed, interconnect bandwidth, and mapping topology, explaining when mapping determines system bottlenecks and how communication improvements translate into inference latency gains.
    • Combined hierarchical mapping search with incremental resource-load updates to make deployment exploration practical at thousands-of-dies scale. Validated placement sensitivity and mapping-selection quality against published silicon measurements and independent cycle-accurate BookSim simulations.
  • 03/2026-09/2026
    Efficient and Robust Long-Horizon LLM Agents through Perturbation-Robust Reinforcement Learning and Trajectory-Anchored Model Pruning
    University of Arizona
    • Investigated perturbation-robust reinforcement learning for long-horizon LLM agents, using sensitivity-aware training perturbations to stabilize policies against compounding action errors. Evaluated robustness to noise, pruning, and quantization on ALFWorld and WebShop.
    • Studied structured FFN pruning with trajectory-anchored recovery, combining teacher-generated interaction histories with distillation on student-generated responses to limit cross-turn error accumulation. Evaluated iterative pruning, recovery, and importance rescoring to preserve agent performance under compression.
  • 08/2024-03/2026
    Sequential Attention Replacement and Sparsity-Guided Knowledge Distillation for Efficient Inference with Pretrained Vision Transformers
    University of Arizona
    • Proposed sequential attention replacement to adapt pretrained vision transformers to in-memory computing, addressing attention's dynamic activation-to-activation operations while retaining the surrounding pretrained architecture.
    • Designed interface-compatible BiLSTM and bidirectional Mamba substitutes and trained them through block-wise distillation to approximate pretrained attention behavior.
    • Showed through controlled layer-group experiments that replacing sparser attention blocks incurs smaller accuracy losses, identifying inference-time sparsity as a useful signal of attention replaceability.
    • Developed sparsity-guided distillation that explicitly sparsifies teacher attention through adaptive token retention, translating this observation into improved replacement fidelity and smaller student-teacher accuracy gaps across DeiT model scales.
